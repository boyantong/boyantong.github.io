I study how work, technology and institutions shape people’s health and everyday lives. My projects bring together social research, field interviews, documentary filmmaking and design.

## Research and writing

### The Social Impact and Cross-cultural Analysis of Short-Video Recommendation Algorithms on TikTok

Boyan Tong · The Frontiers of Society, Science and Technology · 2026 · 8(3), 9–20

This narrative literature review examines how TikTok and Douyin recommendation systems interact with users and shape information exposure. It traces the feedback between user behaviour and algorithmic prediction, and considers both opportunities for participation and risks involving addiction, polarisation and bias. The paper also discusses how generative AI complicates content governance and uses a cross-cultural framework to compare the two platforms. It argues for greater transparency and attention to cultural differences in platform governance.

[Read the published article](https://doi.org/10.25236/FSST.2026.080302)

### Macro-level Governance Environment and Occupational Health: A Comparison of Pneumoconiosis Policies

Boyan Tong · Independent research

This project combines international policy comparison, Chinese provincial panel data from 2007 to 2023, and institutional case studies to examine occupational health governance. A two-way fixed-effects model explores associations between industrial structure, pollution, public services and reported tuberculosis incidence. The quantitative outcome is tuberculosis reporting, not a direct measure of pneumoconiosis. Policy and case analysis then examines the links between workplace protection, employment records, occupational disease diagnosis and compensation, asking where workers lose access to support.

### User Experiences with Chronic-Disease Self-Management Apps: A Qualitative Analysis of Google Play and Apple App Store Reviews

Boyan Tong · Pioneer Academics research

Using Python to collect app-store reviews and inductive qualitative coding, I examined eight apps for managing chronic conditions. The study explores what users value, including longitudinal records and information sharing, alongside barriers such as small text, inflexible recording schedules, paywalls and disruptive updates. It asks how these design choices affect people whose physical abilities change as their symptoms worsen. Because app-store reviewers form a self-selected sample, the findings describe reported experiences rather than the prevalence of problems among all patients.

## Wearable dust detector

I developed a wearable dust detector and companion app for stone-cutting workers. The project combines PM2.5 and PM10 monitoring with light, sound and vibration alerts, making changes in dust levels easier to notice during work. The app organises readings over time and presents concentration trends, device status and guidance. Sixty devices were distributed free of charge, and the project recorded more than 170 high-dust alerts during one month. The design grew out of my fieldwork on pneumoconiosis and my interest in making occupational exposure more visible.

## Documentary and installation

### Borrowed Health

Borrowed Health is a 12-minute documentary centred on three people living with pneumoconiosis: Zhang Hua, Tao Lujun and Xu Guohe. Through their accounts, the film follows how industrial work continues to shape daily movement, family relationships and financial obligations long after exposure has ended. My work included developing the interview framework and script, interviewing participants and directing the project. The film connects the material achievements of labour with the health costs that workers and their families continue to bear.

### BREATH·LEDGER

BREATH·LEDGER is an interactive installation inspired by my pneumoconiosis fieldwork in Pingxiang. A family photograph is obscured by a layer of digital dust. As a visitor approaches and puts on a mask, the dust clears; when the visitor leaves, it returns. The interaction makes a largely overlooked injury visible and invites reflection on the tension between earning a living and protecting one’s health, and on how responsibility for care falls on individuals and families.

## Fieldwork and health communications

### Pneumoconiosis fieldwork in Pingxiang

In Pingxiang, I interviewed former miners, patients and family members, clinicians, community workers and local officials. Their accounts led me to investigate how employment histories, access to diagnosis and compensation shape life after occupational illness. The reporting also follows the work of rehabilitation centres and the effects of illness on caregiving, household income and children’s education.

### 萍乡尘肺病记忆——会长 医生 书记 病人

孟广轩、童博岩 · China Development Brief · 18 March 2026

Co-authored field reporting brings together memories of mining work and accounts of life with pneumoconiosis. It examines the difficulty of obtaining compensation when employment records are missing, the social role of rehabilitation centres and the burden of long-term care within families.

### China Lifeline Express Foundation

During my internship with the China Lifeline Express Foundation, I observed cataract care in a train hospital in Inner Mongolia and supported health communications work in Beijing. I used Excel and SPSS to clean and code 1,059 patient records, translated a 24-page promotional booklet and helped draft fundraising materials. This experience extended my interest in healthcare access by showing how mobile services connect patients with treatment closer to their communities.
